NormGym: Testing Whether Multi-Agent Systems Can Learn Unspoken Social Rules

An interactive benchmark measures an agent's ability to infer community expectations entirely through social interaction.

When humans enter a new community, they quickly absorb implicit customs and social boundaries without explicit manuals. To evaluate whether autonomous agents possess similar capabilities, researchers introduced NormGym, an interactive benchmark testing normative competence in multi-agent environments. Rather than testing whether language models recognize predefined ethics, NormGym forces newcomer agents to infer unwritten, arbitrary social norms through observation and interaction while pursuing separate goals. Current frontier models struggle substantially, reaching only a 55.3% joint success rate and revealing that models frequently fail to extrapolate social sanctions to novel scenarios.

AI agents operating in human groups must be capable of inferring implicit social boundaries rather than relying solely on hardcoded rules.

Decision Context: Teaching AI Agents to Retain Behavioral Corrections

Forge_dc compiles human feedback into persistent operational rules to end repetitive prompt corrections.

A persistent limitation of conversational AI agents is their inability to retain behavioral corrections across sessions, requiring developers to repeatedly intervene on identical mistakes. A new system titled Forge Decision Context (forge_dc) tackles this challenge by treating procedural memory separately from factual recall. The system extracts behavioral signals from user interventions and failed tool invocations, translates them into imperative rule directives, and selectively surfaces them at relevant operational checkpoints. In benchmark evaluations across 71 scenarios, this targeted rule injection elevated agent compliance to 95% while drastically reducing context token overhead.

Solving procedural memory in autonomous agents ensures users do not have to correct the exact same mistake across multiple working sessions.
