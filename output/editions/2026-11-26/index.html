Partial Routing: Trace-Tuned Subtask Delegation Cuts Cost and Boosts Agent Accuracy

New research shows frontier models solve complex benchmarks better when delegating distinct subtasks to cheaper helpers.

A new research paper explores 'partial routing,' a method where a frontier lead model delegates specific subtasks to a smaller, cost-effective worker model during agentic execution. Tested on benchmarks like ViBench and Terminal-Bench 4.0, partial routing raised task performance beyond lead-only baselines while reducing inference expenses by up to 19%. Rather than delegating entire prompts, breaking workflows into measured subtasks prevents lead degradation and establishes a template for resource-efficient autonomous agents.

Granular delegation within multi-step agentic workflows drastically lowers inference costs while simultaneously improving overall problem-solving success.

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis Holds in Jacobian-Space Across Vision and Language

New findings show vision and language transformers converge on shared internal representations up to an orthogonal rotation.

By mapping the gradient directions that most strongly drive output classifications (J-space), they discovered that vision and text models converge on nearly identical semantic spaces, differing merely by an orthogonal rotation. A single rotation fitted on a small vocabulary subset successfully mapped unseen classes, suggesting deep geometric harmony across multimodal architectures.

Evidence of universal representations across modalities suggests that distinct AI models naturally construct unified models of the physical world.
